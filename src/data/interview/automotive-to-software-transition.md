# What made you transition from automotive work into software development?

- **Category:** Introduction and Background
- **Source ID:** automotive-to-software-transition
- **URL:** https://www.lst97.dev/chat

My interest in technology actually started in high school, where I studied Information Technology. I found that I really enjoyed understanding how systems worked, diagnosing problems, and finding solutions. At the time, automotive training was less commonly available in Hong Kong, and I also wanted to explore something outside software, so I decided to study and work in the automotive field. I particularly enjoyed the diagnostic side of automotive work because it involved systematic troubleshooting—understanding symptoms, identifying possible causes, testing them, and narrowing the problem down. However, automotive work can also be very physically demanding, and over time I decided to return to an area that I had always been interested in: computer science. Even while working in automotive, I continued learning programming in my free time. I studied fundamentals such as C++ and Assembly to better understand how software works at a lower level. That was also interesting to me because modern vehicles contain many embedded computer systems that are ultimately built on similar computing concepts. In the end, I realised that I genuinely enjoyed both fields, but software development gave me more opportunities to continuously learn and solve different types of problems. I also think my automotive background has helped me become a better developer because it trained me to approach problems methodically, diagnose issues based on evidence, and understand some of the fundamentals behind electronics, automation, and robotics.
