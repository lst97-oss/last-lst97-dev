# What technical decision in one of your projects would you make differently today, and why?

- **Category:** System Design and Architecture
- **Source ID:** technical-decision-differently-today
- **URL:** https://www.lst97.dev/chat

One example would be the system I originally built for Best Maker. At the beginning, I designed the quotation and invoice functionality specifically around that company's requirements. At the time that was reasonable because Best Maker was the only customer and the immediate goal was to solve their business problem. After working on it further, I realised that much of the underlying functionality — company profiles, customers, quotations, invoices, line items and document generation — is not actually specific to Best Maker. If I were designing it again today, I would separate the generic invoicing and quotation domain from the Best Maker-specific presentation and business rules much earlier. Rather than putting Best Maker-specific assumptions throughout the system, I would design the core so that different organisations could have their own company information, templates, configuration and permissions. Best Maker could then use that common platform while still having its own quotation and invoice styling or custom integrations. I would not necessarily build a full SaaS platform from day one, because that could also be premature over-engineering. But I would create clearer boundaries between reusable business functionality and customer-specific behaviour so that turning it into a multi-tenant product later would be much easier. The positive side is that I originally tried to keep the project modular and maintainable, so I can reuse a significant amount of the domain knowledge and application logic. However, moving from a Best Maker-specific Next.js application toward a more reusable architecture still requires migration work. The main lesson I took from that project is to distinguish between what is genuinely customer-specific and what represents a reusable business domain, while still avoiding building abstractions before there is evidence that they are needed.
